## Proposed title

**Readout Failure and Preserved Task Information after Grokking with Muon**

## Proposed abstract

Transformers trained on modular arithmetic with Muon on hidden matrices and AdamW on embeddings and readout can lose training and test accuracy after grokking. Across five unnormalized trajectories, adjacent checkpoint interventions localize most acute damage to the readout: updated features remain useful with the preceding head, and fresh decoders trained only on the original training examples recover 98.20 to 100% held-out accuracy. The actual updates initially descend training loss and then overshoot. The readout displacement acting on the inflated feature mean closely reproduces the acute damage. Frozen-state audits identify cross-entropy derivative error and show how Adam converts a class-common error into class-discriminating updates. **[PRIMARY_ARITHMETIC_ENDPOINT_SENTENCE: state the five-seed stock versus accurate-CE outcomes from step 6000 through 30000.]** Exact global basis equivalence fails the measured geometry tests. An RMS-normalized extension collapses even with accurate loss arithmetic, through an embedding update. The results establish retained task information and a numerical readout-failure pathway in the original configuration, together with an explicit architecture boundary.

### Arithmetic error and the state preceding failure

The trained models enter a regime where the arithmetic used for cross-entropy changes its derivatives substantially. On identical float32 logits, the stock logit-gradient error relative to an accurate float64 reference rises from 0.97 to 2.23% at step 6000 to 10.56 to 21.86% at step 15000 across the five seeds. The corresponding readout-gradient errors at step 15000 are 18.95 to 43.42%. This comparison changes the loss derivative while retaining the same float32 network for backpropagation. Correct-class entries can become exactly zero while wrong-class entries remain nonzero, violating the exact zero-sum derivative identity. Reconstructing only the correct-class entry from the negative sum of stock wrong-class entries restores this identity and closely matches the accurate reference on fixed logits.

The trajectories also develop large feature and classifier means. Between step 15000 and the immediate pre-event checkpoints, the full-grid feature-mean norm rises from 1,087 to 1,486 across seeds to 10,374 to 12,203. ### From gradient error to class preference

Exact softmax cross-entropy is invariant to adding the same scalar to every class logit. This implies a zero class sum for each example's logit gradient. A violation can produce a class-common readout-gradient component. Under a scalar SGD update, such a component changes a shared logit offset. Adam's coordinatewise scaling depends on class-specific optimizer state and can convert it into a class-discriminating update. ### Readout overshoot in the unnormalized model

For immediately adjacent checkpoints, let H0 and H1 denote the preceding and following residual matrices, and W0 and W1 their readouts. Applying W0 to H1 preserves or improves the preceding held-out accuracy in every seed. Applying W1 to H0 reproduces most of the collapse. The current-gradient contribution to the terminal Adam readout update is damaging in all five seeds under a decomposition that holds the update denominator fixed. Replacing the final gradient by an accurate derivative alone leaves all five acute failures. These results identify a sensitive state that has already developed before the triggering update.

With H0 held fixed, the accurately evaluated training-loss derivative along W1 minus W0 is negative at the beginning of the step in all five seeds. The full displacement nevertheless increases training loss sharply. Selecting a nonnegative scale on the prespecified grid using training loss alone chooses 0.1 in four seeds and 0.01 in the remaining seed. This establishes overshoot in the preceding fixed-feature readout problem. The statement is reference-specific: using H1, seed 3's direction has a positive derivative already at scale zero.

A separate full-network check gives a negative accurate training-gradient dot product with the complete captured parameter displacement in every seed. Each hidden, embedding, and readout contribution is negative. Scaling the full displacement to 0.001 or 0.01 reduces training loss in all five cases; the full displacement sharply increases it. The interpolation endpoints exactly reproduce all saved parameters. Thus the complete observed update is locally descending and overshoots at its actual finite scale.

Let mu be the mean of H1 over training examples and dW equal W1 minus W0. The exact identity

    H1 dW = (H1 - mu) dW + mu dW

separates the centered-feature and mean contributions. Starting from H1 W0, adding only mu dW gives held-out accuracies of 32.11%, 9.42%, 0.93%, 53.34%, and 66.07% for seeds 0 through 4, compared with 31.87%, 9.09%, 0.93%, 53.60%, and 66.33% under the full displacement. Adding only the centered-feature contribution yields 95.26%, 92.48%, 100%, 87.20%, and 98.46%. The same training mean is used for held-out examples. This local intervention connects the inflated feature mean to the destructive update: mu dW is an input-independent class-logit vector that can dominate otherwise useful predictions. In seed 2 it sends 99.955% of held-out examples to one class, while the centered contribution preserves 100% accuracy.

### What survives the failure

Fresh linear decoders recover 98.20 to 100% held-out accuracy at the selected collapsed checkpoints. Fits use original training examples, whitening estimated from those examples, and regularization selected inside that training subset. Healthy-reference probes obtain 99.40 to 100%. These results establish substantial preserved linear task information, with smaller decoder gaps remaining in three seeds. They complement the adjacent swaps by measuring recoverability beyond a particular inherited head. One-time repairs have limited persistence under the tested continuing optimizer states, so instantaneous recovery and sustained stabilization are reported separately.

## Replacement Fourier and basis paragraphs

For a residual representation H and readout W, a joint transformation H to H A and W to inverse(A) W preserves logits for any invertible A supported by the architecture. This is a symmetry of the parameterization. An actual trajectory follows an exact global representation change of basis only if its two residual matrices satisfy H1 equal to H0 A on the input domain. Our full-domain least-squares diagnostics, held-out fits, matched healthy intervals, and planted network transformations find discrepancies far above the measured numerical controls for the recorded pairs. Approximate transports frequently repair predictions, providing a useful quantitative description of representation/readout compatibility. Immediately preceding heads provide an essential control because distant references also measure ordinary coadaptation.

An invertible residual map preserves whether each Fourier coefficient vector is exactly zero. Orthogonal maps additionally preserve its squared norm. General invertible maps can change power rankings and threshold-defined dominant-frequency sets. Accordingly, the manuscript distinguishes exact support from power concentration and from accuracy after Fourier filtering through the current head. A filtered pathway can fail under its current readout while substantial task information remains accessible to another trained readout. We therefore use the term filtered-pathway failure for that operational measurement and report fresh-decoder recoverability separately.
