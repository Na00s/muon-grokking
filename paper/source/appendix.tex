\section{Setup details and operational definitions}
\label{app:setup}

\input{tables/definitions}
\input{tables/configs}

Each example is the token sequence $[a,b,=]$; the vocabulary contains $p+1=114$ tokens and the output layer $p=113$ classes. The ordered operand grid contains $p^2=12{,}769$ examples; a fixed random $30\%$ (unless stated) forms the training set, generated once from the run seed and shared by all matched comparisons. The original runs load a saved initial state from disk, evaluate every $100$ steps, and checkpoint every $1{,}000$. Depth-1 and depth-2 runs last $100{,}000$ steps and depth-4 runs $300{,}000$.

Each block holds four two-dimensional matrices (fused QKV $384\times128$, attention output $128\times128$, MLP input $512\times128$, MLP output $128\times512$), $196{,}608$ parameters; with embeddings and unembedding the depth-1 model has $226{,}048$ parameters. Depth is varied by appending blocks under nested initialization, so deeper models begin from a strict superset of the shallower model's initial state. Muon maintains an exponential moving average of the gradient with $\beta=0.95$, forms a Nesterov-style update, normalizes it by its Frobenius norm, applies five quintic Newton--Schulz iterations with coefficients $(3.4445,-4.7750,2.0315)$, scales by $\sqrt{\max(1,\text{rows}/\text{cols})}$, and applies decoupled weight decay; the implementation refuses any parameter that is not a matrix. All AdamW groups use $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$.

Table~\ref{tab:definitions} gives the operational definitions for the original experiments; evaluations occur every $100$ steps, so a sustained criterion holds across a $500$-step window. Training sweeps and matched-branch experiments run at a single seed on an accelerator backend that is not bitwise deterministic across processes; the five-seed replications run on CPU, where execution is bitwise deterministic, so the Muon and Stable Muon arms are the same trajectory until the freeze fires and agree to a maximum absolute difference of zero over the seventy-four to seventy-five evaluations preceding it. The seed sets the initialization and the train/test split together, so the five seeds are five draws of the problem instance.

The fastest AdamW configuration ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-2}$, $\mathrm{wd}=1.0$) is unusable: $918$ sub-threshold evaluations at this seed, and across seeds three of five never reach sustained $95\%$, the two that do record $805$ and $956$, and all five end within a tenth of a point of chance. \section{Ablating the normalized-orthogonalization path}
\label{app:ablation}

\input{tables/nons}

The ablation removes the Frobenius normalization and the Newton--Schulz iteration together, leaving momentum, shape scaling, and weight decay; the update carries the magnitude and direction of the buffer, and the learning rate must rise to $0.3$ or $1.0$ for the model to learn at all. Table~\ref{tab:nons}: six ablated runs generalize, none records a post-grokking evaluation below $95\%$, and all six end in a non-finite loss, in float64 as well as float32, at terminal steps spanning $22{,}889$ to $38{,}955$ and at hidden norms that set no failure threshold. Muon on the matched run reaches a minimum of $29.49\%$ and recovers to $96.48\%$, with thirteen sub-threshold evaluations before the earliest ablated failure: the ablated runs span the window in which Muon oscillates and none of them does. The two failures differ in kind, and the ablation collapses spectral dispersion from $326.09$ effective conjugate pairs to $4.11$ while leaving the task-aligned family at $100\%$ sufficiency (Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}).

\section{Fourier decomposition and intervention suite}
\label{app:fourier}

\input{tables/families}
\input{tables/interventions}
\input{tables/families-result}

For a chosen point in the forward pass the model runs on all $p^2$ operand pairs, the states form a tensor $X\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p\times d}$, and an orthonormal two-dimensional DFT over the operand axes gives $\widehat{X}[k,l,:]$ with power $\lVert\widehat{X}[k,l,:]\rVert^2$. Because $X$ is real, modes occur in conjugate pairs and every filter retains or removes both members together; transforms run in single precision on CPU. The families of Table~\ref{tab:families} are pairwise disjoint and exhaust the grid, and addition and subtraction are disjoint because $p$ is odd. Power fractions are normalized against non-constant power; filters that retain the addition family retain the full diagonal including the constant mode, since the bias-free unembedding turns a constant component into a fixed logit offset. Removing a family and retaining its complement are the same measurement, and they agree exactly everywhere, checking the filters.

Table~\ref{tab:families-result} applies sufficiency and ablation to each family at forty-three solved checkpoints spanning five seeds and the Muon, Stable Muon, and AdamW regimes. Equal-power relocation ranges $0.88\%$ to $15.04\%$ over $430$ replicates. Taken in descending order of power, reaching $95\%$ requires two to four conjugate pairs at AdamW checkpoints (median $3$), against three to seventeen at Muon (median $12.5$) and eight to nineteen at Stable Muon (median $11$). Along the AdamW trajectory of \S\ref{sec:outvoted}, the five strongest pairs give $4.91\%$ at step $3{,}000$, $19.48\%$ at $20{,}000$, $70.73\%$ at $30{,}000$, and $100\%$ at $37{,}000$, while the family's share rises from $1.05\%$ through $11.08\%$ and $33.20\%$ to $70.62\%$, reaching $90.51\%$ by step $100{,}000$; test accuracy over those steps is $0.30\%$, $2.08\%$, $15.97\%$, and $100\%$.
