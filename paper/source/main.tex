\begin{abstract}
Under the standard routing that assigns Muon the hidden weight matrices and AdamW the embeddings and output head, Muon reaches the grokking threshold on modular arithmetic in fewer steps. Every configuration in our original Muon sweep subsequently loses generalization; some also lose training accuracy. AdamW at larger step sizes collapses too. Freezing the interface suppresses collapse in matched branches. At five captured transitions, the readout update is sufficient for the acute loss of accuracy, while fresh linear decoders recover $98.20$--$100\%$ from collapsed representations. The measured pairs fail tests of exact global basis equivalence. Correcting cross-entropy arithmetic prevents joint train/test collapse through step $30{,}000$ in all five matched branches; the original failing updates locally descend the accurate loss and overshoot at their applied scale. An RMS-normalized counterexample instead fails through its embeddings despite corrected cross-entropy gradients. Fourier filtering separates failure through the current readout from \emph{circuit masking}, where the task-aligned family with its retained constant mode solves the task perfectly and is outvoted in the full model; rescaling the family restores accuracy. At a captured collapse, dominant-frequency membership is unchanged and the power distribution remains nearly identical. These spectral statistics alone do not establish that a learned circuit still functions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Mechanistic studies of grokking combine Fourier analysis with functional interventions. \citet{nanda2023progress} identify a Fourier algorithm and track its formation with restricted and excluded losses. We study what happens to such representations after generalization. At a captured collapse on modular addition, the dominant-frequency membership of the task-aligned family is unchanged and its pair-power distribution retains a cosine similarity of $0.9899$ (Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}). These spectral statistics remain stable as accuracy fails. One possible explanation is that the representation changes basis while the readout fails to compensate. We test this directly: fresh decoders recover the task, while measured residuals fail exact global basis-equivalence tests. The results separate retained task information, accuracy through the current readout, and coordinate equivalence.

We use Muon \citep{jordan2024muon} with the routing used in large-scale training \citep{liu2025muon}: hidden weight matrices receive orthogonalized momentum, while embeddings and the output head remain with AdamW. We reproduce faster grokking under this routing in our modular-arithmetic setup \citep{tveit2025muon,wang2026active}. Every configuration in our stock-arithmetic sweep on $(a+b)\bmod 113$ subsequently loses generalization. Training accuracy falls with test accuracy in captured failure intervals. AdamW at larger step sizes also collapses, with severity differing across optimizers. We observe instability across the tested moduli, widths, training fractions, operations, and depths 1, 2, and 4. Earlier grokking leaves more of the training budget available to study these later failures through matched branches and interventions.

The measured unnormalized failures localize to the representation--readout interface. The logits depend on the final residual $h$ and the unembedding $W_U$ through $W_Uh$, so $(Rh,W_UR^{-1})$ implements the same function for any invertible $R$ \citep{elhage2021framework}. This parameterization symmetry alone does not specify an optimizer trajectory. Branching from bit-identical states, freezing either side prevents the imminent failure over the tested window. Anchoring the embeddings and readout after circuit formation yields no sub-threshold evaluation across five runs totaling $451{,}400$ post-grokking steps; five paired seeds also remain stable while their unfrozen arms record $137$ to $321$ such evaluations each. Adjacent-checkpoint interventions then isolate the acute readout update, and fresh decoders establish that substantial task information survives it.

Filtering the representation to the Fourier family the task selects separates two failure modes. In \emph{circuit failure}, the filtered family fails through the current readout; this is a pathway-level measurement, compatible with recovery by a fresh decoder. In \emph{circuit masking}, the family plus the retained constant mode solves the task perfectly while the full model fails: read through the unembedding, the family casts a correct vote on every example, larger on the examples the model answers wrongly, and is outvoted by the rest of the representation. Rescaling the family alone, with nothing retrained, restores the model. In the measured AdamW trajectory, the family is already highly accurate in isolation before the model generalizes, and model accuracy rises alongside its share of representational power.

Numerical controls connect these events to prior work on softmax collapse and finite-precision loss spikes \citep{prieto2025grokking,liu2026glitching}. Correcting cross-entropy from step $6{,}000$ prevents joint train/test collapse through step $30{,}000$ in five matched branches. The captured failing updates are local descent directions for the accurate loss, but their full applied steps overshoot. Gain-free RMS normalization exposes a boundary: an accurate-loss run still collapses through an embedding update. The numerical contribution and the acute locus therefore depend on the architecture.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Phenomenon.} All original Muon sweep configurations lose test accuracy after grokking; joint train/test failures occur in a subset. AdamW also collapses at larger step sizes (\S\ref{sec:speed}).
\item \textbf{Mechanism.} Matched freezes, adjacent-update interventions, fresh decoders, and arithmetic corrections identify an acute readout failure with surviving task information in the unnormalized model, and an embedding failure in an RMS control (\S\ref{sec:localize}).
\item \textbf{Taxonomy.} Filtering to the task-selected Fourier family separates circuit failure from circuit masking; masking is a competition on amplitude that rescaling alone resolves, with the reverse transition observed during grokking (\S\ref{sec:outvoted}).
\item \textbf{Methodology.} Stable spectral statistics can coexist with functional failure. Decoder and basis tests distinguish retained information from exact coordinate equivalence (Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}, \S\ref{sec:outvoted}, Appendix~\ref{app:basis}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task.} We train on $(a+b)\bmod p$, and on $(a-b)\bmod p$ where stated, using token sequences $[a,b,=]$ and $p$ output classes. Defaults are $p=113$ and a fixed random $30\%$ training split of the $p^2$ operand grid. Training is full batch with cross-entropy. Original sweeps evaluate every $100$ steps; diagnostic branches use finer grids where specified. The arithmetic follow-ups add the monitoring in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}.

\textbf{Architecture.} The model is a decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=128$, four heads, MLP width $512$, ReLU, sequence length $3$, every projection bias-free, and no normalization layers. An invertible change of residual basis can be absorbed by the matrices that write to and read from it. This is a symmetry of the parameterization; optimizer updates need not preserve it. The gain-free RMS control restricts this symmetry to $O(d)$. Depth is varied by appending blocks under nested initialization.

\section{Muon groks faster, and the solutions do not hold}
\label{sec:speed}

All nine Muon configurations in a $3\times3$ sweep of the hidden group reach sustained generalization, against seven of eleven AdamW configurations, with mean times of $13{,}011$ and $30{,}486$ steps; the mean is computed over AdamW's successes alone, so the factor of $2.34$ is conservative. On the selected settings the advantage is $1.54$ at the median over five seeds, and Muon's grokking step spans one hundred steps where AdamW's spans seven thousand seven hundred. The ratios reach $4.8$--$4.9$ under half width and a smaller modulus. AdamW never groks in the $20\%$ training variant, giving a lower bound of $6.6$; depth also increases the advantage (\S\ref{sec:depth}).

All nine Muon configurations lose test accuracy after grokking; six have a logged evaluation with both train and test accuracy below $95\%$. None is strictly stable: sub-threshold counts range from $2$ to $592$, post-grokking minima reach $0.78\%$, and the counts have no monotone relationship to either hidden hyperparameter. Four of the seven grokking AdamW configurations also collapse, with severity rising monotonically in the learning rate, from none at $10^{-3}$ to $918$ sub-threshold evaluations at $10^{-2}$. Sweeping the readout learning rate over a factor of five and its weight decay from zero to $0.5$ changes the timing and depth of collapse and eliminates none of it; the zero-decay case removes the weight-decay pull on the readout and leaves the failure in place. Across five seeds neither selected setting is strictly stable: Muon records $137$ to $321$ sub-threshold evaluations on every seed with minima to $16.27\%$, and the AdamW reference records one or two on four of five, with a minimum of $27.59\%$. The tested Muon subtraction run also loses test accuracy; its matched AdamW run stays above threshold. Full sweeps, the baseline-selection protocol, and generality variants are in Appendix~\ref{app:sweeps}.

\subsection{The gradient vanishes and the hidden step does not}
\label{sec:quiet}

Figure~\ref{fig:quiet} measures steps $44{,}010$--$44{,}700$ of an instrumented rerun. Training loss is near $1.5\times10^{-7}$, with hidden and non-hidden gradient norms of order $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-5}$. Muon normalizes its momentum before a fixed Newton--Schulz iteration, reducing sensitivity to its magnitude. Across this window, the log--log slope of the applied hidden step against the hidden gradient norm is $-0.03$. Slopes for the embedding and readout steps against the combined non-hidden norm are about $+1.5$, with $R^2$ across the three fits ranging from $0.84$ to $0.98$. The hidden step and decay largely cancel; their net displacement grows by $64\%$ from step $44{,}100$ to $44{,}700$ while the gradient-driven step changes little. By step $44{,}700$, root-mean-square displacement per parameter from the branch state is $0.073$ for hidden parameters and $0.0091$ for non-hidden parameters, a factor of $8.0$. These measurements establish different displacement scales; their causal interpretation requires the numerical controls in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}. In the separate ten-step replay used for Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} and Table~\ref{tab:quiet}, training loss rises by $3.8\times10^{8}$ across one interval, and training and test accuracy fall from $100\%$ to $21.12\%$ and $19.04\%$.

Table~\ref{tab:branches} reports branches from a common state, each run for $2{,}000$ steps with a different subset of parameters held fixed. Freezing either group suppresses the failure for the whole window; freezing the token or position embedding delays it without preventing it; freezing the unembedding prevents it over the same window, and leaving only the hidden matrices and the unembedding free reproduces it. With the non-hidden parameters frozen, Muon continues to apply hidden updates near $0.146$ while loss remains near $1.5\times10^{-7}$.

These branches establish suppression over a finite window; freezing also changes subsequent gradients and optimizer history. To isolate the failing update, Figure~\ref{fig:followup}a swaps features and heads between adjacent checkpoints at five captured events. In every seed, the old head retains or improves accuracy on the updated features, while the new head sharply lowers accuracy on the old features. Longer branches still show coadaptation: each decodes its own addition-filtered representation and fails on the other's, and initially identical heads reach cosine similarity $-0.028$. These observations establish readout sensitivity and surviving information. Exact global basis equivalence fails for the measured pairs, despite successful recovery of planted transformations. We hold the token embedding, the position embedding, and the unembedding constant from roughly two thousand steps after sustained generalization, while hidden matrices continue to receive Muon updates; we call the configuration \emph{Stable Muon}. Across five runs spanning the main condition, $p=97$, training fraction $20\%$, and width $64$, a total of $451{,}400$ post-grokking steps and $4{,}519$ evaluations, no evaluation falls below $95\%$, and in every run the post-grokking minimum is the accuracy at the evaluation that first met the criterion. \subsection{Numerical controls and the failing step}
\label{sec:arithmetic}

Five matched branches replace stock cross-entropy with a cancellation-resistant \texttt{log1p} evaluation from step $6{,}000$, after grokking and measurable derivative error. Through step $30{,}000$, none has a joint train/test failure below $90\%$; minimum measured test accuracy is $98.86\%$, and all finish at $100\%$. Every-update training checks trigger test evaluation below threshold, detecting every joint event. Brief test-only excursions can fall between grid evaluations. From step $15{,}000$ to $20{,}000$, accurate loss, target-derivative repair, and per-example zero-sum gradient projection each give $0/5$ failures (Appendix~\ref{app:arithmetic}). Projection retains substantial derivative error while restoring the class-sum identity, connecting the result to numerical feature inflation \citep{liu2026glitching}.

Before the captured failures, full-grid feature-mean norms reach $10{,}374$--$12{,}203$. An intervention using the following representation's training mean nearly reproduces each failure with only the mean-dependent part of the readout update. Replacing gradients only at the terminal update still fails in all five seeds, implicating accumulated state. Each complete update has negative directional derivative under the accurate training loss, and scales $0.001$ and $0.01$ lower that loss; the full update overshoots (Appendix~\ref{app:mean-update}).

One matched subtraction pair reproduces prevention through $30{,}000$ steps. A gain-free RMS pair bounds its scope: with accurate loss, test accuracy falls from $96.21\%$ to $0.88\%$. Updating only embeddings gives $0.94\%$, while updating only the readout retains $96.21\%$. Accurate arithmetic therefore leaves a distinct embedding failure possible; this one-seed counterexample does not estimate its prevalence (Appendix~\ref{app:normalized}).

\section{The task selects the family; the update rule shapes its spread}
\label{sec:family}

We view each cached state as a function of the operand pair. An orthonormal two-dimensional Fourier transform over all $p^2$ pairs partitions its modes into six disjoint families: non-constant \emph{addition} $(k,k)$, \emph{subtraction} $(k,-k)$, $a$-only, $b$-only, the constant mode, and generic interaction. Family \emph{sufficiency} retains that family's modes; \emph{ablation} removes them. Both interventions retain the constant mode when targeting a non-constant family. Unless stated otherwise, Fourier accuracies and solved-checkpoint selection ($\geq95\%$) use the full operand grid. Appendix~\ref{app:fourier} specifies the interventions.

\textbf{The task-aligned family is sufficient at the sampled solved checkpoints.} In Table~\ref{tab:swap}, the addition family reaches $100.00\%$ at every selected addition checkpoint. Removing it leaves near-chance accuracy, while removing the subtraction family has little effect. Under matched initialization, configuration, and code path, training on $(a-b)\bmod113$ exchanges these roles: $(k,-k)$ reaches $100\%$ and $(k,k)$ gives chance accuracy. This consistency across the sampled optimizers and seeds complements prior evidence for shared algorithmic families with variation in the learned circuits \citep{chughtai2023toy}.

\textbf{Frequency and phase matter within the family.} Transporting the family onto other diagonal frequencies at matched power gives $2.64\%$. Randomizing phase per conjugate pair gives $0.19\%$, and randomizing phase per channel gives $0.78\%$. These interventions establish sensitivity to frequency assignment and phase. Two branches diverging from a bit-identical state each decode their own addition-filtered representation at $100\%$ and the other's at chance, showing dependence on the associated readout.

\textbf{The update rule shapes the spread.} On matched runs the Muon representation occupies $326$ effective conjugate pairs of the non-constant spectrum against AdamW's $5$, leaving the task-aligned family holding $28\%$ of non-constant power against AdamW's $91\%$, and the contrast replicates across seeds and inside the family itself (Figure~\ref{fig:grid}; Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}). Ablating the normalization and orthogonalization together collapses the count to $4$, past AdamW's, while leaving the family untouched at $100\%$ sufficiency: the task selects the family, the update rule shapes how widely power spreads, and the two are separable.

\section{A circuit can be present, correct, and outvoted}
\label{sec:outvoted}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figure5_modes.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Left:} task-aligned family accuracy (including the retained constant mode) against full-model accuracy over $98$ checkpoints from the original studies, both evaluated through the current readout. \emph{Circuit failure} denotes failure of this filtered pathway; \emph{circuit masking} sits at the lower right, where the family solves the task and the model does not. At $95$ of $98$ checkpoints the family alone is at least as accurate as the full model, and the three exceptions exceed it by under a point. \textbf{Right:} rescaling the non-constant task-aligned component of the masked checkpoint by $\alpha$, with the rest of the representation and the readout untouched.}
\label{fig:modes}
\end{figure}

Filtering the final residual to the task-aligned family and decoding measures its accuracy through the current readout; comparison with the unfiltered model measures interference from the remainder. The two measurements separate two failures (Figure~\ref{fig:modes}). In \emph{circuit failure}, filtering gives $2.65\%$ and then chance: this filtered pathway fails, and the family has fallen out of the top twenty power pairs almost entirely. Fresh-decoder recovery in \S\ref{sec:branches} shows why such failure alone cannot establish loss of task information. In \emph{circuit masking}, filtering gives $100\%$ while the full model reaches $45.85\%$: the isolated circuit is perfect, and the representation it sits in overwhelms it. One matched trajectory passes through failure, full recovery, and masking; its frozen counterpart retains high filtered accuracy and substantial family power (Appendix~\ref{app:branchtraj}).

\textbf{Masking is a competition on amplitude.} Because the unembedding is linear and bias-free, the correct-class margin decomposes exactly into the task-aligned contribution and the remainder (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). Against the full model's highest-scoring incorrect class, the non-constant family contributes a positive margin on every example in both branches, including every example the masked model answers wrongly, and on those examples its margin is higher, $+6.08$ against $+5.30$. The failures coincide with stronger opposition from the remainder: the remainder contributes negatively on more than nine tenths of examples in both branches, and in the control an opposition of $-6.43$ outvotes a task-aligned term of $+5.72$. Rescaling the task-aligned component by $\alpha$, with nothing retrained, raises the masked model monotonically on the sampled grid from $45.85\%$ to at least $95\%$ at $\alpha=2.55$ and $99.91\%$ at $\alpha=6$: the intervention changes the balance of the two logit contributions without retraining. Family power share is a descriptive measure of this change.

\textbf{Family power during grokking.} Along an AdamW trajectory sampled every thousand steps, the addition family reaches $95.14\%$ at step $3{,}000$, when the model scores $0.30\%$ and the family holds $1.05\%$ of non-constant power. Ablating the family leaves near-chance accuracy at every sampled checkpoint. Its share rises to $70.62\%$ at the test-accuracy jump, alongside greater concentration within the family. This trajectory is consistent with the cleanup account of \citet{nanda2023progress}. Power share provides a descriptive measure of that transition; the margin decomposition and rescaling intervention establish amplitude competition at the masked checkpoint.

\textbf{Spectral stability and functional change.} Exact nonzero Fourier support is invariant under an invertible residual-basis map; power and its rankings are invariant under orthogonal maps. General invertible maps can change dominant-frequency membership. A jointly compensated basis change preserves predictions. At the collapse in Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}, dominant-frequency Jaccard is $1.0000$ and power cosine is $0.9899$, while the filtered pathway's mean margin changes from $+18.14$ to $-46.85$. Over this interval the unembedding moves by $1.285$ of its norm and the addition-filtered residual by $0.392$; the old head on new features scores $26.31\%$, against $5.74\%$ for the reverse. Adjacent swaps provide finer localization (Figure~\ref{fig:followup}a). Accuracy returns to $99.90\%$ within $300$ steps with a changed power allocation. In the depth-4 pair, patching the frozen branch's addition component of the final MLP write into the control gives $99.99\%$ with the frozen head and $5.05\%$ with the control head. These results show readout sensitivity; exact basis equivalence requires the separate tests in Appendix~\ref{app:basis}.

\section{Construction and alignment separate in depth}
\label{sec:depth}

The speed advantage is $2.22\times$ at depth 2 and exceeds $5.7\times$ at depth 4, where AdamW never sustains generalization within the budget. The AdamW configuration stable at depth 1 records $556$ sub-threshold evaluations at depth 2. Averaging five phase perturbations at each stage, the first accuracy loss exceeding half the baseline occurs at an MLP activation in ten of eleven solved checkpoints and an MLP write in the remaining one. Earlier attention writes can be necessary under zeroing while insensitive to these frequency and phase interventions. This distinction limits localization by ablation alone. Across $41$ checkpoints selected by full-grid accuracy, direct decoding of a post-block residual reaches $95\%$ only at the final block and never exceeds $17.97\%$ earlier. Thus compatibility with the final readout is concentrated at the last block in the sampled models. At depth 4, anchoring the interface cuts sub-$90\%$ evaluations about twenty-fold on an exact-state matched branch (Appendix~\ref{app:depth}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Grokking on modular arithmetic.} \citet{power2022grokking} introduced the phenomenon. \citet{nanda2023progress} reverse-engineered a Fourier algorithm, used causal ablations, and introduced restricted and excluded losses to track circuit formation and cleanup. \citet{zhong2023clock} show that hyperparameters select qualitatively different algorithms; \citet{chughtai2023toy} find shared algorithmic structure alongside variation across learned circuits.

\textbf{Failure after grokking.} \citet{thilak2022slingshot} report cyclic instability under adaptive optimizers; \citet{varma2023explaining} describe ungrokking under reduced data; \citet{prieto2025grokking} identify softmax collapse and a loss-minimization direction that scales logits. \citet{liu2026glitching} connect finite-precision violations of the logit-gradient class-sum identity to feature inflation and loss spikes, and test projection-based corrections. Our arithmetic controls build on this account; adjacent swaps and fresh decoders locate the acute functional failure and surviving information. Joint train/test collapse differs from the perfect-training regime of anti-grokking \citep{prakash2025grokking}, while the accurate-loss RMS event establishes a separate architectural boundary.

\textbf{Muon.} \citet{jordan2024muon} introduced the optimizer and \citet{liu2025muon} deployed it at scale under the routing used here. \citet{tveit2025muon} and \citet{wang2026active} report faster grokking in their tested settings; the latter studies orthogonalization and the speed/stability tradeoff with iteration count, including accuracy dips. We examine the five-iteration configuration over longer horizons. \citet{ruan2026muon} and \citet{wang2025tailend} report higher effective rank and a more isotropic weight spectrum under Muon. Our Fourier measurements concern dispersion over operand frequencies, and the joint ablation tests the contribution of normalization and orthogonalization.

\textbf{Representation and readout.} \citet{elhage2021framework} describe residual-stream basis freedom. \citet{anthes2023diagnosing} identify readout misalignment as a major component of forgetting under new tasks. \citet{chou2026twospeeds} study relative representation and readout learning speeds, while \citet{gomezjurado2026longdelay} finds a decoder bottleneck in one-step Collatz prediction. Here the task stays fixed and the dissociation occurs after generalization: joint train/test failure can coexist with high accuracy under a fresh decoder.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The tests establish surviving task information, with training-only decoders reaching $98.20$--$100\%$, while rejecting exact global basis equivalence for the measured pairs. Arithmetic corrections suppress joint failures over fixed horizons; captured updates overshoot accurate local descent directions. Together with adjacent swaps and the feature-mean intervention, these results support a numerical contribution to acute readout failure. The relative contributions of feature growth and optimizer history remain unresolved.

The accurate-loss RMS embedding failure limits this account. The main controls cover five selected trajectories; the operation and normalization extensions use one seed each. Longer horizons and brief test-only excursions remain unresolved, and AdamW collapses limit attribution specifically to Muon. Fourier filtering identifies functional components using the complete task grid; it does not by itself identify the full weight-level algorithm. Spectral statistics, linear decodability, and accuracy through the current readout answer different questions about what survives collapse.

Matched freezes and arithmetic branches verify shared states before intervention.
