\begin{abstract}
Post-grokking collapse can destroy accuracy while preserving decodable task information. We study modular-arithmetic transformers trained with Muon on hidden weights and AdamW on embeddings and the output head. At five captured unnormalized transitions, adjacent swaps localize the acute failure to the head update. Its mean-dependent logit term reproduces each failing test accuracy within $0.33$ percentage points. Training-only linear decoders recover $98.20$--$100\%$ at collapsed checkpoints. Corrected cross-entropy prevents joint train/test collapse through step $100{,}000$ in five matched unnormalized branches; four prospective RMS-normalized runs still fail through embedding updates. The captured unnormalized updates locally descend accurate loss and overshoot at full scale. Finally, task-aligned Fourier filtering averages logits over inputs sharing the answer, allowing a memorizer to achieve perfect filtered accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generalization acquired during grokking can later collapse. In five captured failures of unnormalized modular-arithmetic transformers, swaps across a single optimizer update localize the acute damage to the readout: the preceding head remains accurate on the updated features, while the new head lowers accuracy on the preceding features. Fresh linear decoders fitted on training examples recover $98.20$--$100\%$ held-out accuracy at selected failed checkpoints. The mean-dependent component of the actual readout displacement nearly reproduces each failure (Figure~\ref{fig:followup}; \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}).

\citet{liu2026glitching} already connect inaccurate cross-entropy derivatives, feature/classifier-mean growth, amplified Adam updates, and numerical corrections. \citet{chou2026twospeeds} use a representation--readout decomposition and linear probes to diagnose readout miscalibration during grokking. We add a joint account of individual post-grokking failures: adjacent-update localization, an intervention on the mean-dependent part of the measured readout displacement, and a test of the task information retained at failed states. These interventions identify the damaging update; the upstream contributions of feature growth and optimizer history remain coupled.

We use Muon \citep{jordan2024muon} with the routing used in large-scale training \citep{liu2025muon}: Muon trains the hidden matrices, while AdamW trains the token and position embeddings and the readout. Our speed and stability comparisons concern the stated configurations, sweeps, and horizons. Matched corrected-cross-entropy continuations remain free of joint train/test failure through step $100{,}000$ in all five unnormalized seeds; a tenfold post-grokking reduction in the hidden learning rate leaves failures in all five. With gain-free RMS, accurate-CE failures replicate in four prospective seeds. Swaps localize each captured transition to embeddings, while failed-state decoder accuracy varies substantially, establishing an architectural boundary for this account.

Fourier measurements support this analysis with a separate qualification. Retaining a full task-aligned family averages representations over inputs with the same answer, supplying enough task structure for a lookup-table memorizer to achieve perfect filtered accuracy. Raw-feature decoders and adjacent swaps assess surviving information independently of this projection. Spectral similarity during a captured collapse is discussed alongside the projection analyses in \S\ref{sec:outvoted}.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Localized failures with decodable information.} At five captured unnormalized transitions, adjacent swaps isolate the readout update. Its mean-dependent displacement nearly reproduces the loss of accuracy, while fresh training-only decoders recover high held-out accuracy (\S\ref{sec:localize}).
\item \textbf{Stabilization and its scope.} Matched continuations compare numerical correction and hidden-learning-rate reduction through $100{,}000$ steps. Four prospective RMS runs establish limits to numerical prevention, readout localization, and retained decodability (\S\ref{sec:arithmetic}).
\item \textbf{Interpretation of Fourier interventions.} An orbit-average identity and memorizer controls explain the task structure supplied by filtering and calibrate its use as evidence for a learned algorithm (\S\ref{sec:family}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task.} We train on $(a+b)\bmod p$, and on $(a-b)\bmod p$ where stated, using token sequences $[a,b,=]$ and $p$ output classes. Defaults are $p=113$ and a fixed random $30\%$ training split of the $p^2$ operand grid. Training is full batch with cross-entropy. Original sweeps evaluate every $100$ steps; diagnostic branches use finer grids where specified. The arithmetic follow-ups add the monitoring in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}.

\textbf{Architecture.} The model is a decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=128$, four heads, MLP width $512$, ReLU, sequence length $3$, every projection bias-free, and no normalization layers. An invertible change of residual basis can be absorbed by the matrices that write to and read from it. This is a symmetry of the parameterization; optimizer updates need not preserve it. The gain-free RMS control restricts this symmetry to $O(d)$. Depth is varied by appending blocks under nested initialization.

\section{Earlier grokking in the tested Muon configurations}
\label{sec:speed}

The configuration-selection criteria differ. AdamW uses the fastest strictly stable configuration in its single-seed sweep; every tested Muon configuration is unstable, so Muon uses the fastest of those configurations (Appendix~\ref{app:sweeps}). Across five seeds at these selected settings, median grokking steps are $8{,}300$ for AdamW and $5{,}400$ for Muon, a $1.54\times$ ratio. The fastest successful single-seed sweep entries reach the criterion at $6{,}300$ and $5{,}400$ steps, respectively, a $1.17\times$ ratio. These summarize different comparisons.

All nine entries in Muon's $3\times3$ hidden-group sweep grok, compared with seven of eleven AdamW entries. Their success-conditional mean times are $13{,}011$ and $30{,}486$ steps, a $2.34\times$ ratio. The grids differ and this mean excludes four unsuccessful AdamW configurations. In the selected five-seed runs, Muon's grokking steps span $100$ steps and AdamW's span $7{,}700$. Ratios reach $4.8$--$4.9$ under half width and a smaller modulus. AdamW never groks in the $20\%$ training variant, yielding a horizon-dependent lower bound of $6.6\times$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sweeps}); the depth comparisons are in \S\ref{sec:depth}.

All nine Muon configurations lose test accuracy after grokking; six have a logged evaluation with both train and test accuracy below $95\%$. None is strictly stable: sub-threshold counts range from $2$ to $592$, post-grokking minima reach $0.78\%$, and the counts have no monotone relationship to either hidden hyperparameter. Four of the seven grokking AdamW configurations also collapse, with severity rising monotonically in the learning rate, from none at $10^{-3}$ to $918$ sub-threshold evaluations at $10^{-2}$. Sweeping the readout learning rate over a factor of five and its weight decay from zero to $0.5$ changes the timing and depth of collapse and eliminates none of it; the zero-decay case removes the weight-decay pull on the readout and leaves the failure in place. Across five seeds neither selected setting is strictly stable: Muon records $137$ to $321$ sub-threshold evaluations on every seed with minima to $16.27\%$, and the AdamW reference records one or two on four of five, with a minimum of $27.59\%$. The tested Muon subtraction run also loses test accuracy; its matched AdamW run stays above threshold. Full sweeps, the baseline-selection protocol, and generality variants are in Appendix~\ref{app:sweeps}.

\section{Localizing the collapse at the interface}
\label{sec:localize}



\subsection{Muon updates remain nearly constant at small gradient norms}
\label{sec:quiet}

Figure~\ref{fig:quiet} measures steps $44{,}010$--$44{,}700$ of instrumented continuation I, sampled every update (Appendix~\ref{app:control-provenance}). Its first recorded training failure is at $44{,}702$. Training loss is near $1.5\times10^{-7}$, with hidden and non-hidden gradient norms of order $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-5}$. Muon normalizes its momentum before a fixed Newton--Schulz iteration, reducing sensitivity to its magnitude. Across this window, the log--log slope of the applied hidden step against the hidden gradient norm is $-0.03$. Slopes for the embedding and readout steps against the combined non-hidden norm are about $+1.5$, with $R^2$ across the three fits ranging from $0.84$ to $0.98$. The hidden step and decay largely cancel; their net displacement grows by $64\%$ from step $44{,}100$ to $44{,}700$ while the gradient-driven step changes little. By step $44{,}700$, root-mean-square displacement per parameter from the branch state is $0.073$ for hidden parameters and $0.0091$ for non-hidden parameters, a factor of $8.0$. These measurements establish different displacement scales; their causal interpretation requires the numerical controls in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}. In the separate ten-step replay used for Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} and Table~\ref{tab:quiet}, training loss rises by $3.8\times10^{8}$ across one interval, and training and test accuracy fall from $100\%$ to $21.12\%$ and $19.04\%$.

\subsection{Numerical controls and the failing step}
\label{sec:arithmetic}

Five matched branches replace stock cross-entropy with a cancellation-resistant \texttt{log1p} evaluation from step $6{,}000$, after grokking and measurable derivative error. Through step $100{,}000$, the original training horizon, none has a joint train/test failure below $90\%$; minimum measured test accuracy is $98.86\%$, and all finish at $100\%$. Every-update training checks trigger test evaluation below threshold, detecting every joint event. Brief test-only excursions can fall between grid evaluations. From step $15{,}000$ to $20{,}000$, accurate loss, target-derivative repair, and per-example zero-sum gradient projection each give $0/5$ failures (Appendix~\ref{app:arithmetic}). Projection retains substantial derivative error while restoring the class-sum identity, connecting the result to numerical feature inflation \citep{liu2026glitching}.

From matched step-$6{,}000$ states, reducing Muon's hidden learning rate from $0.03$ to $0.003$, with stock cross-entropy and trainable embeddings and readout, gives $5/5$ joint failures by step $100{,}000$ (Figure~\ref{fig:followup}c). This particular tenfold reduction is insufficient to stabilize the five matched continuations. With unchanged weight-decay coefficients, it also reduces the hidden decoupled weight-decay step (Appendix~\ref{app:lr-reduction}).

\textbf{The mean-dependent head update reproduces the acute failure.} Before the captured failures, full-grid feature-mean norms reach $10{,}374$--$12{,}203$. Appendix~\ref{app:mean-timeline} provides descriptive timelines from the separate archived Muon and AdamW seed study. Let $h_1$ be the post-update residual, $W_{U,0}$ the preceding head, $\Delta W_U$ its actual displacement, and $\mu$ the training-set mean of $h_1$. Starting from the rescued logits $W_{U,0}h_1$, the head-induced change decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
\Delta z=\Delta W_U(h_1-\mu)+\Delta W_U\mu.
\label{eq:followup-mean}
\end{equation}
The same training-estimated $\mu$ is used for all test examples. Adding only $\Delta W_U\mu$ nearly reproduces each failure (Table~\ref{tab:mean-update}); its test accuracy differs from the full update by at most $0.33$ percentage points. The centered term retains $87.20$--$100\%$ test accuracy and at least $99.09\%$ training accuracy. This evaluation-only intervention establishes local sufficiency of the mean-dependent term, a class-dependent logit offset shared by all examples. Feature growth and optimizer history remain coupled before the failing update.

Replacing gradients only at the terminal update still fails in all five seeds. Each complete update has negative directional derivative under the accurate training loss, and scales $0.001$ and $0.01$ lower that loss; the full update overshoots (Appendix~\ref{app:mean-update}).

The corrected subtraction run also remains free of joint failure through $100{,}000$ steps. With gain-free RMS and accurate CE from initialization, all four prospective CPU seeds fail after grokking, at steps $14{,}271$--$15{,}789$. Embedding-only updates reproduce joint failure in each; retaining the old embeddings with updated hidden matrices and readout gives $98.41$--$100\%$ test accuracy. \section{Task-aligned Fourier projections}
\label{sec:family}

We view each cached state as a function of the operand pair. An orthonormal two-dimensional Fourier transform over all $p^2$ pairs partitions its modes into six disjoint families: non-constant \emph{addition} $(k,k)$, \emph{subtraction} $(k,-k)$, $a$-only, $b$-only, the constant mode, and generic interaction. Family \emph{sufficiency} retains that family's modes; \emph{ablation} removes them. Both interventions retain the constant mode when targeting a non-constant family. Unless stated otherwise, Fourier accuracies and solved-checkpoint selection ($\geq95\%$) use the full operand grid. Appendix~\ref{app:fourier} specifies the interventions.

\textbf{The filter supplies task structure.} Retaining all $(k,k)$ modes and the constant mode is exactly the orbit average
\begin{equation}
(P_{\mathrm{add}}h)(a,b)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}h(a+t,b-t),
\label{eq:orbit-average}
\end{equation}
with indices modulo $p$. Every donor has the same sum, and the linear readout commutes with this average. For subtraction, the shifts are $(a+t,b+t)$. A lookup-table predictor that outputs the correct one-hot vector on training examples and zero elsewhere becomes perfect after addition filtering whenever every answer orbit contains a training example. Appendix~\ref{app:fourier-control} verifies this control on the experimental splits. Full-family filtered accuracy thus measures the answer-conditioned average of the predictor; high accuracy alone leaves the learned algorithm unidentified.

\textbf{Task-aligned projection preserves accuracy at the sampled solved checkpoints.} In Table~\ref{tab:swap}, the addition family reaches $100.00\%$ at every selected addition checkpoint. Removing it leaves near-chance accuracy, while removing the subtraction family has little effect. Under matched initialization, configuration, and code path, training on $(a-b)\bmod113$ exchanges these roles: $(k,-k)$ reaches $100\%$ and $(k,k)$ gives chance accuracy. These results characterize the task-aligned projection and family removal; both interventions retain the constant mode. The averaging identity also produces this operation dependence, so identifying shared learned algorithms requires additional evidence \citep{chughtai2023toy}.

\textbf{Frequency and phase matter within the family.} Transporting the family onto other diagonal frequencies at matched power gives $2.64\%$. Randomizing phase per conjugate pair gives $0.19\%$, and randomizing phase per channel gives $0.78\%$. These interventions establish sensitivity to frequency assignment and phase. The memorizer controls in Appendix~\ref{app:fourier-control} also exhibit sparse filtered sufficiency and perturbation sensitivity, so these tests leave algorithm identification unresolved. Two branches diverging from a bit-identical state each decode their own addition-filtered representation at $100\%$ and the other's at chance, showing dependence on the associated readout.



\textbf{The update rule shapes the spread.} On matched runs the Muon representation occupies $326$ effective conjugate pairs of the non-constant spectrum against AdamW's $5$, leaving the task-aligned family holding $28\%$ of non-constant power against AdamW's $91\%$, and the contrast replicates across seeds and inside the family itself (Figure~\ref{fig:grid}; Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}). These operand-frequency measurements complement prior work on Muon's spectral properties \citep{ruan2026muon,wang2025tailend,wang2026active}. Ablating the normalization and orthogonalization together collapses the count to $4$, past AdamW's, while task-family filtered accuracy remains $100\%$. These comparisons show a change in spectral dispersion. The filtered accuracy retains the averaging interpretation above.

\section{Interference and spectral change}
\label{sec:outvoted}

The task-aligned projection can fail with the model or remain accurate while the full model fails (Figure~\ref{fig:modes}). At a masked checkpoint, full-grid filtered accuracy is $100\%$ and full-model accuracy is $45.85\%$. Rescaling the non-constant task-aligned component raises accuracy to $99.91\%$ without retraining. This is an intervention on a task-informed projection. Appendix~\ref{app:projection-detail} gives the margin decomposition, the scaling curve, and the early AdamW observation of $95.14\%$ full-grid filtered accuracy alongside $0.30\%$ held-out native accuracy. The memorizer control in \S\ref{sec:family} limits what these filtered accuracies establish.

\section{Construction and alignment separate in depth}
\label{sec:depth}

At depth $2$, Muon and AdamW reach the grokking criterion at $2{,}300$ and $5{,}100$ steps within a $100{,}000$-step budget ($2.22\times$). At depth $4$, Muon reaches it at $52{,}600$; AdamW remains censored at $300{,}000$, giving a lower bound of $5.7\times$. The AdamW setting selected for stability at depth $1$ records $556$ sub-threshold evaluations at depth $2$. At depth $4$, an exact-state freeze of embeddings and readout at step $126{,}200$ reduces sub-$90\%$ evaluations from $1{,}353$ to $66$ and raises mean post-branch accuracy from $48.07\%$ to $97.50\%$ through step $300{,}000$ ($1{,}738$ post-branch evaluations at $100$-step intervals; Appendix~\ref{app:depth}).

Across $41$ solved checkpoints, intermediate post-block residuals decoded by the final head never exceed $17.97\%$, and only the final block reaches $95\%$. This measures compatibility with that particular head. Appendix~\ref{app:depth} retains the stagewise perturbation analyses, their five-perturbation averages, and the complete depth results.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Grokking on modular arithmetic.} \citet{power2022grokking} introduced the phenomenon. \citet{nanda2023progress} reverse-engineered a Fourier algorithm, used causal ablations, and introduced restricted and excluded losses to track circuit formation and cleanup. \citet{zhong2023clock} show that hyperparameters select qualitatively different algorithms; \citet{chughtai2023toy} find shared algorithmic structure alongside variation across learned circuits.

\textbf{Numerical instability and adaptive updates.} \citet{thilak2022slingshot} report cyclic instability under adaptive optimizers; \citet{varma2023explaining} describe ungrokking under reduced data. \citet{prieto2025grokking} identify softmax collapse and a loss-minimization direction that scales logits. \citet{liu2026glitching} connect cross-entropy class-sum errors to feature/classifier-mean growth and amplified Adam updates, and test corrections to this numerical feedback. Our matched arithmetic branches extend this evidence to Muon/AdamW routing. Adjacent swaps localize individual failures, and intervention on the mean-dependent part of the actual readout displacement tests its local sufficiency. Joint train/test failure also differs operationally from the perfect-training regime of anti-grokking \citep{prakash2025grokking}.

\citet{wortsman2024smallscale} reproduce attention-logit growth and output-logit divergence in small transformers at high learning rates, and show that independently scaling learning rate and weight decay reduces learning-rate sensitivity. This motivates treating our ordinary LR reduction as a joint change in update size and decay. \citet{molybog2023adam} analyze Adam updates with growing magnitude and weak alignment to descent directions in large-model training. Our captured updates have negative accurate-loss directional derivatives but overshoot at their full scale, providing a measured finite-step qualification for these events.

\textbf{Muon.} \citet{jordan2024muon} introduced the optimizer and \citet{liu2025muon} deployed it at scale under the routing used here. \citet{tveit2025muon} and \citet{wang2026active} report faster grokking in their tested settings; the latter studies orthogonalization and the speed/stability tradeoff with iteration count, including accuracy dips. We examine the five-iteration configuration over longer horizons. \citet{ruan2026muon} and \citet{wang2025tailend} report higher effective rank and a more isotropic weight spectrum under Muon. Our Fourier measurements concern dispersion over operand frequencies, and the joint ablation tests the contribution of normalization and orthogonalization.

\textbf{Representation and readout.} \citet{elhage2021framework} describe residual-stream basis freedom. \citet{anthes2023diagnosing} identify readout misalignment as a major component of forgetting under new tasks. \citet{chou2026twospeeds} use same-split linear probes, geometry, and readout-learning diagnostics to separate representation quality from classifier calibration during grokking and double descent. \citet{gomezjurado2026longdelay} finds a decoder bottleneck in one-step Collatz prediction. Here the task stays fixed: probes at captured post-grokking failures are combined with adjacent-update swaps and a measured-displacement intervention. Initialization and memorization controls calibrate how much linear accessibility precedes the failure.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Training-only decoders reach $98.20$--$100\%$, while exact global basis equivalence fails for the measured pairs. Captured updates overshoot accurate local descent directions. Adjacent swaps, the feature-mean intervention, and arithmetic corrections support a numerical contribution to acute readout failure. The relative contributions of feature growth and optimizer history remain unresolved.

The accurate-loss RMS cohort exhibits embedding-localized failures and variable failed-state decodability. The unnormalized controls cover five selected trajectories; the operation extension uses one seed, and the RMS study adds four prospective seeds to a separately identified historical pilot. Behavior beyond $100{,}000$ steps and brief test-only excursions remain unresolved; AdamW collapses limit Muon-specific attribution. Full-family Fourier filtering supplies answer-orbit structure and mixes training and test inputs. The memorizer control rules out inferring an arithmetic algorithm from perfect filtered accuracy alone. Accuracy can collapse through a damaging readout update while useful task information survives, and corrected arithmetic stabilizes the tested unnormalized runs through step $100{,}000$.
