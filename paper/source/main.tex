\begin{abstract}
Post-grokking collapse can destroy accuracy while preserving decodable task information. We study modular-arithmetic transformers trained with Muon on hidden weights and AdamW on embeddings and the output head. At five captured transitions, adjacent swaps localize the acute failure to the head update. Its mean-dependent logit term reproduces each failing test accuracy within $0.33$ percentage points. Training-only linear decoders recover $98.20$--$100\%$ at collapsed checkpoints. Corrected cross-entropy prevents joint train/test collapse through step $100{,}000$ in five matched branches; an RMS-normalized model still fails through an embedding update. The captured updates locally descend accurate loss and overshoot at full scale. Finally, task-aligned Fourier filtering averages logits over inputs sharing the answer, allowing a memorizer to achieve perfect filtered accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generalization acquired during grokking can later collapse. We follow individual failures in modular-arithmetic transformers to identify the damaging update and determine what information survives. At a captured failure, dominant-frequency membership is unchanged and the task-family power distribution retains cosine similarity $0.9899$ (Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}). Fresh decoders recover high accuracy from collapsed representations.

We use Muon \citep{jordan2024muon} with the routing used in large-scale training \citep{liu2025muon}: hidden matrices receive orthogonalized momentum, while embeddings and the output head remain with AdamW. Prior work reports faster Muon grokking and stability tradeoffs \citep{tveit2025muon,wang2026active}, connects inaccurate cross-entropy derivatives to feature inflation and amplified updates \citep{liu2026glitching}, and uses representation/readout decompositions and linear probes to study grokking \citep{chou2026twospeeds}.

Adjacent swaps isolate the readout update in the unnormalized model: the preceding head retains accuracy on updated features, and training-only decoders recover $98.20$--$100\%$ at collapsed checkpoints. The mean-dependent head update nearly reproduces each failure. Matched freezes and corrected cross-entropy branches test prevention, while an RMS control identifies an embedding failure with accurate loss derivatives. The role of numerical error and the parameter group responsible for collapse differ between the two architectures.

Full-family Fourier filtering averages representations over operand pairs with the same answer. A lookup-table memorizer achieves perfect filtered accuracy despite chance-level held-out predictions. We therefore interpret filtering and rescaling as interventions on a task-informed projection. Raw-feature decoders and adjacent swaps establish surviving information independently of that projection.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Acute failure and surviving information.} Adjacent swaps isolate damaging readout updates in five unnormalized trajectories; training-only decoders recover high held-out accuracy, and a feature-mean intervention nearly reproduces each failure (\S\ref{sec:localize}).
\item \textbf{Numerical mechanism and architectural boundary.} Matched arithmetic corrections test prevention over fixed horizons. Captured updates overshoot accurate local descent directions, and an accurate-loss RMS control fails through its embeddings (\S\ref{sec:arithmetic}).
\item \textbf{Scope of Fourier evidence.} An averaging identity and memorizer controls delimit the interpretation of full-family filtering and rescaling (\S\ref{sec:family}, \S\ref{sec:outvoted}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task.} We train on $(a+b)\bmod p$, and on $(a-b)\bmod p$ where stated, using token sequences $[a,b,=]$ and $p$ output classes. Defaults are $p=113$ and a fixed random $30\%$ training split of the $p^2$ operand grid. Training is full batch with cross-entropy. Original sweeps evaluate every $100$ steps; diagnostic branches use finer grids where specified. The arithmetic follow-ups add the monitoring in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}.

\textbf{Architecture.} The model is a decoder-only transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=128$, four heads, MLP width $512$, ReLU, sequence length $3$, every projection bias-free, and no normalization layers. An invertible change of residual basis can be absorbed by the matrices that write to and read from it. This is a symmetry of the parameterization; optimizer updates need not preserve it. The gain-free RMS control restricts this symmetry to $O(d)$. Depth is varied by appending blocks under nested initialization.

\section{Muon groks faster, and the solutions do not hold}
\label{sec:speed}

All nine Muon configurations in a $3\times3$ hidden-group sweep reach sustained generalization, against seven of eleven AdamW configurations. Mean times are $13{,}011$ and $30{,}486$ steps, a $2.34\times$ ratio, with AdamW's four unsuccessful runs excluded. On the selected settings the advantage is $1.54$ at the median over five seeds, and Muon's grokking step spans one hundred steps where AdamW's spans seven thousand seven hundred. The ratios reach $4.8$--$4.9$ under half width and a smaller modulus. AdamW never groks in the $20\%$ training variant, giving a lower bound of $6.6$; depth also increases the advantage (\S\ref{sec:depth}).

All nine Muon configurations lose test accuracy after grokking; six have a logged evaluation with both train and test accuracy below $95\%$. None is strictly stable: sub-threshold counts range from $2$ to $592$, post-grokking minima reach $0.78\%$, and the counts have no monotone relationship to either hidden hyperparameter. Four of the seven grokking AdamW configurations also collapse, with severity rising monotonically in the learning rate, from none at $10^{-3}$ to $918$ sub-threshold evaluations at $10^{-2}$. Sweeping the readout learning rate over a factor of five and its weight decay from zero to $0.5$ changes the timing and depth of collapse and eliminates none of it; the zero-decay case removes the weight-decay pull on the readout and leaves the failure in place. Across five seeds neither selected setting is strictly stable: Muon records $137$ to $321$ sub-threshold evaluations on every seed with minima to $16.27\%$, and the AdamW reference records one or two on four of five, with a minimum of $27.59\%$. The tested Muon subtraction run also loses test accuracy; its matched AdamW run stays above threshold. Full sweeps, the baseline-selection protocol, and generality variants are in Appendix~\ref{app:sweeps}.

\section{Localizing the collapse at the interface}
\label{sec:localize}



\subsection{Muon updates remain nearly constant at small gradient norms}
\label{sec:quiet}

Figure~\ref{fig:quiet} measures steps $44{,}010$--$44{,}700$ of an instrumented rerun. Training loss is near $1.5\times10^{-7}$, with hidden and non-hidden gradient norms of order $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-5}$. Muon normalizes its momentum before a fixed Newton--Schulz iteration, reducing sensitivity to its magnitude. Across this window, the log--log slope of the applied hidden step against the hidden gradient norm is $-0.03$. Slopes for the embedding and readout steps against the combined non-hidden norm are about $+1.5$, with $R^2$ across the three fits ranging from $0.84$ to $0.98$. The hidden step and decay largely cancel; their net displacement grows by $64\%$ from step $44{,}100$ to $44{,}700$ while the gradient-driven step changes little. By step $44{,}700$, root-mean-square displacement per parameter from the branch state is $0.073$ for hidden parameters and $0.0091$ for non-hidden parameters, a factor of $8.0$. These measurements establish different displacement scales; their causal interpretation requires the numerical controls in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}. In the separate ten-step replay used for Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} and Table~\ref{tab:quiet}, training loss rises by $3.8\times10^{8}$ across one interval, and training and test accuracy fall from $100\%$ to $21.12\%$ and $19.04\%$.

\subsection{Numerical controls and the failing step}
\label{sec:arithmetic}

Five matched branches replace stock cross-entropy with a cancellation-resistant \texttt{log1p} evaluation from step $6{,}000$, after grokking and measurable derivative error. Through step $100{,}000$, the original training horizon, none has a joint train/test failure below $90\%$; minimum measured test accuracy is $98.86\%$, and all finish at $100\%$. Every-update training checks trigger test evaluation below threshold, detecting every joint event. Brief test-only excursions can fall between grid evaluations. From step $15{,}000$ to $20{,}000$, accurate loss, target-derivative repair, and per-example zero-sum gradient projection each give $0/5$ failures (Appendix~\ref{app:arithmetic}). Projection retains substantial derivative error while restoring the class-sum identity, connecting the result to numerical feature inflation \citep{liu2026glitching}.

From matched step-$6{,}000$ states, reducing Muon's hidden learning rate from $0.03$ to $0.003$, with stock cross-entropy and trainable embeddings and readout, gives $5/5$ joint failures by step $100{,}000$ (Figure~\ref{fig:followup}c). This reduction did not match the stabilization from corrected arithmetic. With unchanged weight-decay coefficients, it also reduces the hidden decoupled weight-decay step (Appendix~\ref{app:lr-reduction}).

\textbf{The mean-dependent head update reproduces the acute failure.} Before the captured failures, full-grid feature-mean norms reach $10{,}374$--$12{,}203$. Let $h_1$ be the post-update residual, $W_{U,0}$ the preceding head, $\Delta W_U$ its actual displacement, and $\mu$ the training-set mean of $h_1$. Starting from the rescued logits $W_{U,0}h_1$, the head-induced change decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
\Delta z=\Delta W_U(h_1-\mu)+\Delta W_U\mu.
\label{eq:followup-mean}
\end{equation}
The same training-estimated $\mu$ is used for all test examples. Adding only $\Delta W_U\mu$ nearly reproduces each failure (Table~\ref{tab:mean-update}); its test accuracy differs from the full update by at most $0.33$ percentage points. The centered term retains $87.20$--$100\%$ test accuracy and at least $99.09\%$ training accuracy. This evaluation-only intervention establishes local sufficiency of the mean-dependent term, a class-dependent logit offset shared by all examples. Feature growth and optimizer history remain coupled before the failing update.

Replacing gradients only at the terminal update still fails in all five seeds. Each complete update has negative directional derivative under the accurate training loss, and scales $0.001$ and $0.01$ lower that loss; the full update overshoots (Appendix~\ref{app:mean-update}).

The corrected subtraction run also remains free of joint failure through $100{,}000$ steps. A gain-free RMS pair bounds its scope: with accurate loss, test accuracy falls from $96.21\%$ to $0.88\%$. Updating only embeddings gives $0.94\%$, while updating only the readout retains $96.21\%$. Corrected cross-entropy therefore leaves a distinct embedding failure possible; this one-seed counterexample does not estimate its prevalence (Appendix~\ref{app:normalized}).

\section{Task-aligned Fourier projections}
\label{sec:family}

We view each cached state as a function of the operand pair. An orthonormal two-dimensional Fourier transform over all $p^2$ pairs partitions its modes into six disjoint families: non-constant \emph{addition} $(k,k)$, \emph{subtraction} $(k,-k)$, $a$-only, $b$-only, the constant mode, and generic interaction. Family \emph{sufficiency} retains that family's modes; \emph{ablation} removes them. Both interventions retain the constant mode when targeting a non-constant family. Unless stated otherwise, Fourier accuracies and solved-checkpoint selection ($\geq95\%$) use the full operand grid. Appendix~\ref{app:fourier} specifies the interventions.

\textbf{The filter supplies task structure.} Retaining all $(k,k)$ modes and the constant mode is exactly the orbit average
\begin{equation}
(P_{\mathrm{add}}h)(a,b)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}h(a+t,b-t),
\label{eq:orbit-average}
\end{equation}
with indices modulo $p$. Every donor has the same sum, and the linear readout commutes with this average. For subtraction, the shifts are $(a+t,b+t)$. A lookup-table predictor that outputs the correct one-hot vector on training examples and zero elsewhere becomes perfect after addition filtering whenever every answer orbit contains a training example. Appendix~\ref{app:fourier-control} verifies this control on the experimental splits. Full-family filtered accuracy thus measures the answer-conditioned average of the predictor; high accuracy alone leaves the learned algorithm unidentified.

\textbf{Task-aligned projection preserves accuracy at the sampled solved checkpoints.} In Table~\ref{tab:swap}, the addition family reaches $100.00\%$ at every selected addition checkpoint. Removing it leaves near-chance accuracy, while removing the subtraction family has little effect. Under matched initialization, configuration, and code path, training on $(a-b)\bmod113$ exchanges these roles: $(k,-k)$ reaches $100\%$ and $(k,k)$ gives chance accuracy. These results characterize the task-aligned projection and family removal; both interventions retain the constant mode. The averaging identity also produces this operation dependence, so identifying shared learned algorithms requires additional evidence \citep{chughtai2023toy}.

\textbf{Frequency and phase matter within the family.} Transporting the family onto other diagonal frequencies at matched power gives $2.64\%$. Randomizing phase per conjugate pair gives $0.19\%$, and randomizing phase per channel gives $0.78\%$. These interventions establish sensitivity to frequency assignment and phase. The memorizer controls in Appendix~\ref{app:fourier-control} also exhibit sparse filtered sufficiency and perturbation sensitivity, so these tests leave algorithm identification unresolved. Two branches diverging from a bit-identical state each decode their own addition-filtered representation at $100\%$ and the other's at chance, showing dependence on the associated readout.



\textbf{The update rule shapes the spread.} On matched runs the Muon representation occupies $326$ effective conjugate pairs of the non-constant spectrum against AdamW's $5$, leaving the task-aligned family holding $28\%$ of non-constant power against AdamW's $91\%$, and the contrast replicates across seeds and inside the family itself (Figure~\ref{fig:grid}; Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}). These operand-frequency measurements complement prior work on Muon's spectral properties \citep{ruan2026muon,wang2025tailend,wang2026active}. Ablating the normalization and orthogonalization together collapses the count to $4$, past AdamW's, while task-family filtered accuracy remains $100\%$. These comparisons show a change in spectral dispersion. The filtered accuracy retains the averaging interpretation above.

\textbf{Masking is a competition on amplitude.} Because the unembedding is linear and bias-free, the correct-class margin decomposes exactly into the task-aligned contribution and the remainder (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). Against the full model's highest-scoring incorrect class, the non-constant family contributes a positive margin on every example in both branches, including every example the masked model answers wrongly, and on those examples its margin is higher, $+6.08$ against $+5.30$. The failures coincide with stronger opposition from the remainder: the remainder contributes negatively on more than nine tenths of examples in both branches, and in the control an opposition of $-6.43$ outvotes a task-aligned term of $+5.72$. Rescaling the task-aligned component by $\alpha$, with nothing retrained, raises the masked model monotonically on the sampled grid from $45.85\%$ to at least $95\%$ at $\alpha=2.55$ and $99.91\%$ at $\alpha=6$: the intervention changes the balance of the two logit contributions without retraining. The rescaled component is $P_{\mathrm{add}}h-\bar h$, with $\bar h$ the global mean. Recovery therefore depends on the task structure supplied by the projection. Family power share is a descriptive measure of this change.

\textbf{Family power during grokking.} Along an AdamW trajectory sampled every thousand steps, the addition family reaches $95.14\%$ full-grid filtered accuracy at step $3{,}000$, when the model's held-out test accuracy is $0.30\%$ and the family holds $1.05\%$ of non-constant power. Ablating the family leaves near-chance accuracy at every sampled checkpoint. Its share rises to $70.62\%$ at the test-accuracy jump, alongside greater concentration within the family. The memorizer control shows why this filtered accuracy alone cannot establish algorithm learning at step $3{,}000$. Increasing power share describes the trajectory and is compatible with the cleanup account of \citet{nanda2023progress}. The margin decomposition and rescaling intervention establish amplitude competition between projected logit components at the masked checkpoint.

\textbf{Spectral stability and functional change.} Exact nonzero Fourier support is invariant under an invertible residual-basis map; power and its rankings are invariant under orthogonal maps. General invertible maps can change dominant-frequency membership. A jointly compensated basis change preserves predictions. At the collapse in Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}, dominant-frequency Jaccard is $1.0000$ and power cosine is $0.9899$, while the filtered pathway's mean margin changes from $+18.14$ to $-46.85$. Over this interval the unembedding moves by $1.285$ of its norm and the addition-filtered residual by $0.392$; the old head on new features scores $26.31\%$, against $5.74\%$ for the reverse. Adjacent swaps provide finer localization (Figure~\ref{fig:followup}a). Accuracy returns to $99.90\%$ within $300$ steps with a changed power allocation. In the depth-4 pair, patching the frozen branch's addition component of the final MLP write into the control gives $99.99\%$ with the frozen head and $5.05\%$ with the control head. These results show readout sensitivity; exact basis equivalence requires the separate tests in Appendix~\ref{app:basis}.

\section{Construction and alignment separate in depth}
\label{sec:depth}

The speed advantage is $2.22\times$ at depth 2 and exceeds $5.7\times$ at depth 4, where AdamW never sustains generalization within the budget. The AdamW configuration stable at depth 1 records $556$ sub-threshold evaluations at depth 2. Averaging five phase perturbations at each stage, the first accuracy loss exceeding half the baseline occurs at an MLP activation in ten of eleven solved checkpoints and an MLP write in the remaining one. Earlier attention writes can be necessary under zeroing while insensitive to these frequency and phase interventions. This distinction limits localization by ablation alone. Across $41$ checkpoints selected by full-grid accuracy, direct decoding of a post-block residual reaches $95\%$ only at the final block and never exceeds $17.97\%$ earlier. Thus compatibility with the final readout is concentrated at the last block in the sampled models. At depth 4, anchoring the interface cuts sub-$90\%$ evaluations about twenty-fold on an exact-state matched branch (Appendix~\ref{app:depth}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Grokking on modular arithmetic.} \citet{power2022grokking} introduced the phenomenon. \citet{nanda2023progress} reverse-engineered a Fourier algorithm, used causal ablations, and introduced restricted and excluded losses to track circuit formation and cleanup. \citet{zhong2023clock} show that hyperparameters select qualitatively different algorithms; \citet{chughtai2023toy} find shared algorithmic structure alongside variation across learned circuits.

\textbf{Failure after grokking.} \citet{thilak2022slingshot} report cyclic instability under adaptive optimizers; \citet{varma2023explaining} describe ungrokking under reduced data; \citet{prieto2025grokking} identify softmax collapse and a loss-minimization direction that scales logits. \citet{liu2026glitching} connect finite-precision class-sum violations to coupled feature/classifier-mean growth, amplified Adam updates, and loss spikes; they test zero-sum corrections and normalization. Our arithmetic controls extend this account under Muon/AdamW routing. Adjacent swaps and the mean-dependent displacement intervention locate acute failures; fresh decoders measure surviving information. Joint train/test collapse differs from the perfect-training regime of anti-grokking \citep{prakash2025grokking}. The accurate-loss RMS control identifies an embedding failure in our normalized setting.

\textbf{Muon.} \citet{jordan2024muon} introduced the optimizer and \citet{liu2025muon} deployed it at scale under the routing used here. \citet{tveit2025muon} and \citet{wang2026active} report faster grokking in their tested settings; the latter studies orthogonalization and the speed/stability tradeoff with iteration count, including accuracy dips. We examine the five-iteration configuration over longer horizons. \citet{ruan2026muon} and \citet{wang2025tailend} report higher effective rank and a more isotropic weight spectrum under Muon. Our Fourier measurements concern dispersion over operand frequencies, and the joint ablation tests the contribution of normalization and orthogonalization.

\textbf{Representation and readout.} \citet{elhage2021framework} describe residual-stream basis freedom. \citet{anthes2023diagnosing} identify readout misalignment as a major component of forgetting under new tasks. \citet{chou2026twospeeds} use same-split linear probes to diagnose readout miscalibration during grokking and study relative representation and readout learning speeds, while \citet{gomezjurado2026longdelay} finds a decoder bottleneck in one-step Collatz prediction. Here the task stays fixed and the dissociation occurs after generalization: joint train/test failure can coexist with high accuracy under a fresh decoder.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Training-only decoders reach $98.20$--$100\%$, while exact global basis equivalence fails for the measured pairs. Captured updates overshoot accurate local descent directions. Adjacent swaps, the feature-mean intervention, and arithmetic corrections support a numerical contribution to acute readout failure. The relative contributions of feature growth and optimizer history remain unresolved.

The accurate-loss RMS embedding failure limits this account. The main controls cover five selected trajectories; operation and normalization extensions use one seed each. Behavior beyond $100{,}000$ steps and brief test-only excursions remain unresolved; AdamW collapses limit Muon-specific attribution. Full-family Fourier filtering supplies answer-orbit structure and mixes training and test inputs. The memorizer control rules out inferring an arithmetic algorithm from perfect filtered accuracy alone. Accuracy can collapse through a damaging readout update while useful task information survives, and corrected arithmetic stabilizes the tested unnormalized runs through step $100{,}000$.
