\subsection{Initialization and memorization decoder controls}
\label{app:decoder-controls}

We apply the same decoder to initialization and one memorization-phase state of each of the five unnormalized trajectories in Appendix~\ref{app:basis}. Checkpoint selection uses the recorded native trajectory: the first of five consecutive scheduled $100$-step training evaluations at or above $99.9\%$, requiring test accuracy below $95\%$ at that state and a step before original grokking. For seeds $0$--$4$, this selects steps $200$, $400$, $200$, $100$, and $200$; the corresponding fifth training checks occur at $600$, $800$, $600$, $500$, and $600$. The selection rule, checkpoint identities, and validation indices were fixed before decoder fitting. The initial registration and its pre-fitting amendment to the paper's sustained-memorization criterion are retained.

Replaying each saved initialization through step $1{,}000$ reproduces the original model, optimizer, and CPU RNG states exactly, as well as all ten scheduled evaluation rows. These replays recover the selected early checkpoints without substituting a new trajectory. Their original five-check grokking starts are $5{,}500$, $5{,}500$, $5{,}500$, $5{,}300$, and $5{,}400$, respectively; the historical speed sweep has its own run identities.

Features are captured by a pre-hook on the readout, with no centering or Fourier filtering. All four checkpoints within each seed use the same internal validation indices. The bias-free multinomial logistic regression starts from zero and fits in float64. Every candidate estimates its uncentered, full-dimensional SVD transform using only its fitting subset, with a relative singular-value floor of $10^{-6}$. Validation cross-entropy selects the penalty from $\{0,10^{-8},10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2}\}$; exact ties select the smaller penalty. The selected model is refitted from zero on all original training examples with a newly fitted transform. L-BFGS-B uses $500$ candidate iterations and $1{,}000$ refit iterations, \texttt{ftol} $=10^{-10}$, \texttt{gtol} $=10^{-7}$, at most three times the iteration budget in function evaluations, $20$ line-search steps, and history size $10$. These settings match the existing decoder procedure.

The selected decoder reaches $0.04$--$0.23\%$ held-out accuracy at initialization and $0.02$--$1.12\%$ at memorization, compared with $99.40$--$100\%$ at the existing references and $98.20$--$100\%$ at the failed states (Table~\ref{tab:decoder-phase-controls}). All $20$ selected refits converge. Unregularized memorization refits also converge; all five initialization refits reach the $1{,}000$-iteration limit, with $0.18$--$0.62\%$ held-out accuracy. Candidate objectives, gradient norms, iteration counts, and termination messages accompany the saved coefficients and transforms. Independently recomputing training and test predictions from these arrays reproduces all $40$ new accuracy measurements.

The early controls support the interpretation that the failed checkpoints retain task information made accessible by training. They leave its precise emergence between checkpoints unresolved. Low early accuracy under this bounded decoder procedure does not establish mathematical absence of task information, and high failed-state accuracy alone does not identify a complete arithmetic algorithm or exact preservation of the earlier representation.
